An octopus is a soft-bodied, eight-limbed marine mollusc belonging to the order Octopoda and class Cephalopoda. There are roughly 300 known species found in oceans all over the world, ranging from shallow tide pools to the deep sea. Key Anatomy & Biology • Eight Arms (No Tentacles): They have eight muscular arms lined with hundreds of sensitive suckers used for touching, tasting, and gripping. • Three Hearts & Blue Blood: They possess three hearts—two pump blood through the gills, and one circulates it to the rest of the body. Their blood is copper-based (haemocyanin), which turns it blue. • Nine Brains: They have a central brain plus a local "mini-brain" in each of their eight arms, meaning about two-thirds of their neurons are in their limbs. • Camouflage & Defense: They change color and skin texture instantly using specialized cells called chromatophores. When threatened, they can squirt a cloud of dark ink and dart away. • Venom: All octopus species are venomous, though most are harmless to humans—except for the tiny, lethal blue-ringed octopus. Diet & Intelligence • Diet: As opportunistic carnivores, they feed mostly on crabs, lobsters, shrimp, and bivalves. • Problem-Solving: They are famously smart, capable of navigating mazes, opening jars, using tools (like coconut shells or rocks), and recognizing individual humans.
